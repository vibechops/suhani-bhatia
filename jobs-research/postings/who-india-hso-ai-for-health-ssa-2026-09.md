Overview of the programme
WHO India Country Office collaborates with the Government of India and relevant stakeholders within the framework of the collaborative Country Cooperation Strategy (CCS), to actively support the development and implementation of national health policies, strategies and plans aiming at promoting access to and utilization of affordable and quality health services and improving financial protection against health-related risks.WHO Country Office for India is supporting the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare to strengthen the existing health systems to cater to the growing needs of mental health and substance abuse, palliative care and elderly care in the country.
Underlying Values and Core Functions of WHO
WHO’s mandate revolves around six (6) leadership priorities specifying (i) advancing universal health coverage (ii) health related sustainable development goals (iii) addressing the challenge of non-communicable diseases and mental health, violence and injuries and disabilities (iv) implementing the provisions of the International Health Regulations (2005) (v) increasing access to quality, safe, efficacious and affordable medical products (vi) addressing the social, economic and environmental determinants of health.  The South – East Asia Region (SEAR) of WHO is made up of 10 countries, with over 1.8 billion people, with India’s population of 1.4 billion. All the Member States of WHO/SEAR (Bangladesh, Bhutan, Democratic People's Republic of Korea, India, Maldives, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Thailand and Timor-Leste), share the common value of the highest attainable standard of health as a fundamental human right. All WHO actions are based on this and rooted in the underlying values of equity, solidarity and participation.
Mission of the WHO Country Office for India
The mission of WHO India is to improve quality of life of the 1.4 billion people in India by supporting the government in eliminating vaccine preventable and other communicable diseases, reducing maternal and neonatal mortalities, promoting healthy lifestyles, addressing determinants of health, preparing and responding to health emergencies and strengthening health systems for universal health coverage.Artificial Intelligence (AI) is increasingly influencing health systems, service delivery, public health intelligence, disease surveillance, research, healthcare management, and health workforce performance. India has emerged as a global leader in digital public infrastructure and is rapidly expanding the use of AI technologies across sectors, including health.The Government of India, through the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, released the Strategy for Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare for India (SAHI) in February 2026. SAHI provides a national framework for the responsible, safe, and inclusive integration of AI across India’s health system, structured around five strategic pillars: Governance, Regulation and Trust; Health Data and Digital Infrastructure; Workforce, Institutional Capacity and Change Management; Research, Innovation and Evidence Generation; and Ecosystem Enablement and Global Leadership.The WHO Country Office for India is supporting national and subnational authorities in advancing responsible, equitable, evidence-based, and sustainable use of AI for health, consistent with WHO guidance on ethics, governance, regulation, evaluation, and implementation of AI technologies. To support implementation of WHO's strategic agenda on AI for Health and strengthen engagement with government and partners, WHO India seeks a dedicated Technical Officer to lead technical work on AI and digital innovation for health systems strengthening.This SSA will serve as the primary WHO focal point within India, responsible for coordinating all country-level engagements with the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW), the National Health Authority (NHA), and other national stakeholders in support of the Strategy for Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare for India (SAHI). The role focuses on translating SAHI’s strategic recommendations into actionable inputs for the development and finalization of the implementation plan, development of foundational elements of the AI ecosystem including governance, evaluation framework and workflow integration guides, ensuring alignment with India’s existing digital health architecture, regulatory environment, and programme priorities and evidence generation.
