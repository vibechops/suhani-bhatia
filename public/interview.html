From zero

A case is not a quiz. It is a 25-minute conversation where they watch how you think when the data is incomplete. That is the Analyst job.

1. What they put in front of you

A short story. A client (government, foundation, NGO, sometimes a company). A messy goal. A constraint: money, 24 months, political risk, missing data. Then: “How would you approach this?”

You do not need the right industry fact. You need a structure, a hypothesis, a number, and a recommendation they can repeat to a partner.

2. Dalberg is not McKinsey

Usual consultingDalberg
ClientOne company P&LGovernment, donor, NGO, sometimes corporate
WinProfit, share, costTake-up, coverage, a person actually receiving the thing
DataCompany filesUgly, late, two sources that disagree
ControlClient can decideClient cannot move the whole system alone
FrameworkRevenue − costYou build it. Their words: no off-the-shelf framework

If you open with “I’d look at revenue and costs,” they know you memorised Victor Cheng and did not read their site.

3. Their five steps. Memorise this, not a 12-box framework.

  1. Understand. Repeat the question. Ask 3 clarifying questions. Name the success metric.
  2. Structure. Pause. Draw 3–4 buckets. Say them numbered. Ask if that is a fair way to split the problem.
  3. Analyse. Pick the bucket that would change the answer. Hypothesis first: “I think the break is documents, not awareness, because…”
  4. Quantify. Use their numbers. Talk while you calculate. Sense-check: “Does 80,000 households on 5 crore feel like ₹625 a household? That is a transfer, not a system.”
  5. Synthesise. 20 seconds. “I would do X, because Y. Risk is Z. Next fact I’d want is…”

4. MECE, without the cult

Mutually exclusive: buckets don’t overlap. Collectively exhaustive: you didn’t forget a whole world.

Bad: awareness, documents, “implementation,” “political will.” Those overlap and one of them means nothing.

Better, pensions:

  • Does the person know a benefit exists?
  • Can they prove they are eligible?
  • Can they complete the application without a tout?
  • Once enrolled, do they actually get paid?

That is a funnel. You have used this on BOCW. Use a funnel whenever the problem is “people should get X and don’t.”

5. How to open (first 90 seconds)

Write this on the top of your page every time:

Situation · Complication · Question

Then say: “Let me make sure I have this. You are [client]. You want [outcome] in [time]. The tension is [constraint]. The question is [decision]. Is that right?”

Then three questions, max:

  • What does success look like, in a number?
  • What is already on the ground?
  • What can this client actually control?

Then: “Give me 20 seconds to structure.” Silence is allowed. They prefer a pause to a word-vomit.

6. How to close

They will say “so what would you recommend?” Do not restart the case.

“I would go with [option]. Because [one fact]. The risk is [one risk]. In week one I would [one test].”

Three sentences. Then stop talking.

7. How they score you

  • Did you structure, or list ideas?
  • Did you pick a metric of success that is receipt, not “awareness”?
  • Did you say a hypothesis before fishing for data?
  • Can they follow your maths?
  • Did you name what you don’t know?
  • Would they put you in front of a Joint Secretary next week?

8. Notes on paper

Landscape. Left half: structure. Right half: numbers. Top line: the question in 8 words. Circle the recommendation when you have it. You will need that circle at minute 28 when your brain empties.

Fit is half the interview

Say these out loud. Reading is not prep. The 90-second timer is the first question they will ask.

Tell me about yourself · 90 seconds

1:30
I work on the last mile of public programmes: a pension, a protection order, a welfare benefit that exists on paper and still does not reach the person. I learned how an organisation actually runs by founding a tuition centre in Delhi and taking it to about a lakh a month: pricing, staffing, retention, cash. Then I went into the field. At Sanchay I sat with households and the BDO’s office on pension delivery. At Transform Rural India I did 14 interviews across six states on why district services are uneven. This summer I was inside Telangana Police’s transgender protection cell, writing for leadership about where the 2019 Act does not become a station-level outcome. I am now at TISS, public policy and law. Dalberg is the job that mix was built for: structure a messy public problem, quantify under bad data, recommend what a client can actually do.

Cut the TISS line if they already have the CV. Do not list every internship. End on the job, not on “I am passionate.”

Why consulting

The assignment you asked for is the work I already like. A donor, 5 crore, 24 months, two options, and what evidence would decide. I want to do that every week, with a team, for a client who can move a system. A paper that does not force a decision is not enough for me.

Why social impact, not BCG

I have sat with people who were eligible for a pension and still not paid. The interesting problem for me is not a company P&L. It is a public promise that dies in an office. I can do the same structuring at a strategy firm. I would be bored by the objective.

Why Dalberg

Three things you say about yourselves, which match how I already work. One: the problems are systemic. The client cannot pull one lever. That is the Telangana cell: a police unit cannot complete a welfare pathway alone. Two: solutions need more than one actor. The Sanchay pension work only moved when the BDO office, the SHG, and the household were in the same conversation. Three: data is scarce. I am used to two sources that disagree, and to saying so. The NFHS agency note is an example: accounts moved, decisions barely did. I would rather name the limit than invent a causal story. India is also the right office. Last-mile welfare, digital public infrastructure, state capability. That is the work I want to be the Analyst on.

If they probe: Advisors, not Design or Data Insight, because I want the problem-structuring seat first. I can work with those teams. I am not applying as a designer.

The issue you are most passionate about

Last-mile social protection. Not “poverty.” Specifically: people the state has already declared eligible, who still do not receive. Pensions, BOCW, a protection order. I care about the conversion from statute to a rupee in a bank account, because that is measurable and it is where programmes quietly fail.

SPAR stories · learn four, not twelve

SPAR: Situation, Problem, Action, Result. 60–90 seconds. One number. One thing you would do differently.

Limited data

Sanchay pensions

S. Two villages. Pension delivery. No clean beneficiary file from the block.

P. I could not wait for a census. Eligible people were still not paid.

A. Interviews plus the BDO office. Mapped where the file died: document, authentication, or last-mile cash. Did not pretend I had a district rate.

R. A barrier note for the organisation, not a slogan. I still cannot quote a coverage percentage I trust. That is the point I would say in the interview: I will not invent a denominator.

Leadership / entrepreneur

Green Apple Academy

S. Tuition centre I founded. Evenings. Neighbourhood acquisition.

P. Quality vs growth. A subject I could not staff honestly.

A. Raised fees when retention allowed. Hired only when I could hand off. Refused a subject rather than fake it. Closed it to go to the next work.

R. About ₹1 lakh a month at peak. The consulting point is not the revenue. It is that a theory of change that cannot price, staff and retain will not deliver. I already learned that at 16–23.

Pushback / judgement

Telangana protection cell

S. Three months. Police leadership audience. The Act is 2019.

P. Easy story: restating the Act. Useless story: a rights slogan.

A. Compared Kerala and Tamil Nadu public architecture. Treated the SOP as a hypothesis about delivery. Kept case records inside the cell.

R. A four-part note: where entitlement does not become an administrative act. I would tell a partner the same: a cell can exist and a person can still be unprotected at the station gate.

Analysis under a constraint

Women’s agency / NFHS

S. Schemes treat Jan Dhan as empowerment.

P. Accounts moved 25.6 points. Decision-making moved 4.7. n is small. Causality is not available.

A. Said so. Used published totals. Did not dress OLS up as a treatment effect.

R. A monitoring recommendation: stop using the account as a synonym for agency. Dalberg likes this instinct. Do not hide the limit.

If they poke the CV

7.6 at TISS. “Not the highest in this pile. TISS this year is methods-heavy. I can walk you through the NFHS note and the cost model. I will not pretend a 9.2.”

BBA, not SRCC / not IIT. “The through-line is the last-mile problem, not the brand of the undergraduate college. Green Apple is the operating proof. TISS is the methods layer.”

CAT / quant anxiety. Do not volunteer it. If they see slowness on case math: “I do programme math in units: rupees per household, coverage, months. I am drilling speed. Talk me through a number and I will stay out loud.”

VIP / HR intern. One line: attrition in Tier 2/3 labour markets. Then go back to delivery work. Do not let the interview become an HR internship.

Questions for them

  • What does an Analyst actually own on a typical India project in the first six months?
  • When two official numbers disagree, how does the team decide whether to collect, estimate, or drop the claim?
  • How much of Mumbai/Delhi work is government vs foundation vs corporate?
  • You ran a webinar on the case process. What do new Analysts still get wrong in month two?

Do not ask about work-life balance in round 1. Do not ask if you can work from home.
